A college sports marketing agency is the partner a brand hires to show up at a named game, on a named campus, with the permissions that game actually requires. It is not a synonym for a tailgate. Collegiate Agency staffs the date.

The football parking-lot format lives on the [college tailgate marketing agency guide](/insights/college-tailgate-marketing-agency). Use that guide when the buy is a tailgate. Use this page when you are deciding what a sports hire includes. A brand can buy a tailgate and a separate watch-party. Those are two dates. They are not one word.

## Which sports date are you actually buying?

Write the sport, the school, the venue, and the day. "Football season" is not a date. A home basketball game, a watch party in a student union, and a tent in a campus lot do not share an approver. The athletics department may control the lot and the concourse. A student organization may control a watch party in a residence hall. The campus facilities office may control the sidewalk between them. Name the one you mean, and name the person who can say yes.

[Campus events](/services/events) cover the staffed hour, from $500 to $500,000. A [brand ambassador program](/services/brand-ambassadors) at $150 per ambassador per month is the right second line when you want posts on the weeks between games, not only the game itself. Those rates are on the [pricing page](/pricing). A quote that says "sports marketing" and will not split the game-day crew from the monthly posts is asking you to pay for both while auditing neither.

The University of Arkansas publishes a [tailgating and game day outdoor use policy](https://policies.uark.edu/fayetteville-policies/avcb/8041.php), revised September 8, 2025. It requires prior university and Learfield approval when a corporate tailgate displays business signage, banners, or marketing items, or charges participants. Signage on tents or wrapped vehicles, and distribution of commercial materials, also need prior Arkansas Athletics approval under that policy. That is one school's rule, read as a public example. It is not a national athletics code, and it does not govern a basketball arena or a different university. Collegiate Agency still expects your brief to name the equivalent approval for the school you picked. If the approval is not in hand, the wraps do not get printed with that school's marks.

## What can the brand show, and what stays off the tent?

School marks, athlete names, and conference marks are not yours because you bought a nearby tent. Do not put a logo you do not have rights to on a cooler, a shirt, or a caption. Do not imply an athlete or a team endorses the brand because fans were nearby. The hero photograph on this page is a NUTRL bar: signage, stocked cans, and a bartender holding a can. It is not a stadium, and it is not a claim that a team asked for the bar. Use a venue photo in the recap only when the date you bought was that venue.

That outdoor frame is one person and a can. It can illustrate a sampling hour next to a game if that was the hour you bought. It cannot stand in for a student section, a score, or a crowd count. Ask the recap to separate the sample count from anything the athletics department reports about attendance. Those numbers belong to different owners.

Table: Tailgate, other game-day formats, and an athletics sponsorship

| Format | Typical approver | What the agency can owe you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tailgate in a campus lot | Campus, athletics, and sometimes a rights holder | The tent, the crew, the count you defined |
| Watch party or student event | The host organization and the building | The hour inside that building, not the broadcast |
| Athletics sponsorship | The department or its rights partner | Only the benefits written in that deal |

If your quote sits in the third row, you may not need a field crew at all. Pay the sponsorship to the rights holder and do not also pay an agency to activate benefits the deal does not include. If your quote sits in the first row, use the tailgate guide linked above as the format checklist. This page does not repeat it.

## What does the crew do on the day?

The crew builds what was approved, stays inside the footprint, and leaves when the policy says to leave. Guests are guests. A bartender in a venue photo is a bartender, not your staff. Fans in team colors are not ambassadors. The recap should say how many samples left the table, or whichever count you wrote, and it should include the approval and the strike time.

![BEATBOX poured into a glass from two cartons](/images/blog/beatbox-poured-behind-bar.jpg)

A pour is a pour. If the sport and the venue do not allow alcohol sampling, that frame is the wrong proof and the wrong plan. Say in the brief whether the product can be sampled at all, and under what age rule. Collegiate Agency will not bring a product the campus or the law will not allow, and it will not describe a dry activation with a bar photograph.

Weather can cancel an outdoor lot. The contract should say whether the fee moves to another game or comes back. A season-long claim with no listed games is not a schedule.

## What do you send before game week?

Send one sheet:

- The school, the sport, the venue, and the date.
- The approvers, including any athletics or rights-holder office.
- What you may put on a tent, a shirt, or a cup.
- The product rule, including whether sampling is allowed.
- The crew, the count, and the strike time.
- What happens if the game or the approval moves.

[Contact Collegiate Agency](/contact) with that sheet. The reply, within one business day, is a tailored plan and firm pricing, or a no if the venue cannot be staffed. College sports marketing is a date on a calendar someone else controls. The agency's job is to show you the permission, the crew, and the count, not to borrow the team's audience in a sentence.

Score the replies on whether the agency named the rights holder. A deck that shows a stadium photo and a student in a jersey has not cleared those marks. Ask for the email or the clause that allows the mark, the sign, and the sample. If the agency cannot produce it, assume the mark is not cleared. Collegiate Agency will not print a school name on a wrap because the school is famous. The wrap waits for the approval, even when the game is close and the art department is impatient.

Keep alcohol, sampling, and giveaways on their own lines in the brief. A game-day crowd is full of people under 21. A product that cannot be handed to them should not be the hero of the hour. Say what the crew may hand to a 20-year-old and what it may not. The recap should be able to show that the rule was followed, not only that the tent was full. A full tent with the wrong product is a problem, not a result.
