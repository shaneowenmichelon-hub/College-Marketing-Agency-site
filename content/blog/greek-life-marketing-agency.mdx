A Greek life marketing agency is the partner you hire when the brand must reach fraternity or sorority members through a named chapter, a council, or a philanthropy event, with permission settled before product ships. A media buy purchases a placement. This purchase is an organization list, a calendar window, and a written rule for what may be handed out.

## What does a Greek life marketing agency do?

The agency's job is to turn "Greek life" into a list you can audit. Each row names the campus, the organization, the date, the product, who approved it, and what comes back. If a proposal says "top houses" and stops there, you do not have a plan yet.

A search for a sorority marketing agency and a search for fraternity marketing for brands are the same buying decision with a different host. Chapters do not share one rulebook. Ask which council the chapter sits on, and which national headquarters must say yes, before you treat them as one buy.

Product in the room is a different job from a logo on a flyer. ## Should you buy a chapter program or a council program?

A chapter program is one organization on one campus. The approvers are the chapter officer who can sign, the campus fraternity and sorority office when the campus requires it, and the national headquarters when the product category is restricted. Collegiate Agency's published placement is this shape: 5 organizations per campus, each with its own media deliverables.

A council program is one step up. The Interfraternity Council (IFC), a College Panhellenic, the National Pan-Hellenic Council (NPHC), or a multicultural council may sell a role in a week it runs, such as a recruitment kickoff, Greek Week, or a council philanthropy. The council officer is not the chapter president. An IFC yes does not authorize a Panhellenic chapter, and a Panhellenic yes does not authorize an NPHC chapter.

Greek life sponsorship for brands fails when the deck says "the community" and the contract names nobody. Ask who signs, and whether that signature binds one chapter or every chapter that opts in. Use a chapter placement when product should move through houses that already gather. Use a council role for one public moment the council owns. Use a staffed activation when you need your own crew and age check. Price those as separate scopes.

## Which Greek calendar should the brief name?

Name the window before the headcount. Recruitment, which some campuses still call rush, is a membership process with its own rules. A philanthropy event is a fundraiser or service day with a beneficiary and a host. A formal, sometimes called a date function, is a closed social with a guest list.

Recruitment sampling is the easiest place to buy the wrong thing. Some councils allow a non-alcohol handout. Some ban outside sponsors for the whole recruitment period. Alcohol does not belong in that window. If the handout is a bag insert rather than a house visit, read the [college welcome bag inserts guide](/insights/college-welcome-bag-inserts) and keep that count off the chapter-placement line.

Philanthropy event sponsorship needs the beneficiary, the venue, and whether the public is invited. A dry daytime event and a nighttime social with alcohol are not the same permission. If the logo is only on a banner and the product never moves, you bought signage. Formals need the guest rule and the venue owner. A hotel, a chapter house, and a campus ballroom are three approvers. ## What alcohol and sponsorship rules apply?

Three layers can say no, and any one of them changes the brief. The chapter's national headquarters sets a risk policy. The council sets recruitment rules and what a council event may accept. The university sets registration, publicity, and where alcohol is possible. A student who likes the brand is not one of those three.

For men's fraternities in the North American Interfraternity Conference, start with the [NIC Alcohol and Drug Guidelines](https://nicfraternity.org/nic-alcohol-drug-guidelines/). That page carries a 2026 copyright and tells member organizations to review their own documents by September 1, 2019. Those organizations enforce their own policies. The NIC does not. In any activity the chapter sponsors, the guidelines say four things that change a brief. No one under the legal drinking age may possess or be provided alcohol. Recruitment, intake, rush, bid night, and new-member activities must be substance free. A chapter must not co-host or co-sponsor an event with an alcohol distributor, though it may rent a licensed and insured third-party vendor. Alcohol must not be bought with chapter funds or with money pooled by members or guests. Ask the national headquarters whether its current policy matches that text, and get the answer in writing before an alcohol brand ships anything.

For College Panhellenic events, read the [NPC position statement on campus safety and risk management](https://npcwomen.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Position-Statement-on-Safety-and-Risk-Management-adopted-2019-amended-2025.pdf). The National Panhellenic Conference adopted it in 2019 and amended it in 2025. College Panhellenic-planned or sponsored events must be alcohol-free, and a College Panhellenic cannot spend its funds on alcoholic beverages. Each member sorority sets its own risk policy. The College Panhellenic does not add extra social rules or judge those violations. A council sponsorship and a chapter social are different documents.

The University of Pittsburgh Office of Fraternity and Sorority Life Policies and Procedures Manual for 2026-2027 shows one campus layer. It says no alcohol shall be present at any recruitment event, and no organization may co-sponsor or hold a recruitment event with another organization, including an alcoholic beverage distributor, where alcohol is given away, sold, or otherwise provided. The manual is [published for recognized chapters at that university](https://www.studentaffairs.pitt.edu/sites/default/files/assets/26-27%20FSL%20Policies%20and%20Procedures%20Manual%20%283%29.pdf). It is not a national code. Ask every other campus for its current manual.

NPHC and multicultural councils are not covered by the NIC or NPC pages above. Get their rule, and the chapter national's rule, on their own letter. Do not copy an IFC answer onto those chapters. If the brand is an alcohol industry member, sampling and advertising are two reviews. Collegiate Agency runs the handoff you approve. It does not replace your counsel's review.

## How do you score a Greek life marketing agency?

Send every agency the same brief. This table does not forecast attendance or sales.

Table: What to ask a Greek life marketing agency before you sign

| Question | Acceptable answer | Walk away if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host | A named chapter or council, and who signs | "Greek life" with no organization named |
| Permission | University, council, and national rule, each marked yes, no, or unknown | A student contact treated as campus permission |
| Calendar | Recruitment, philanthropy, or formal, with dates | A semester label and no dates |
| Product | What may be handed out, sampled, or only displayed | Alcohol at recruitment, or a product the rules forbid |
| Scope | Chapter placement, a council role, or a staffed event, on separate lines | One fee that mixes all three and will not say which |
| Closeout | Date, organizations, units, exceptions, and photo rights | A recap with no definition of a count |

Lock the same rows before you sign:

- Confirm which costs are the published rate, an event package, or a quote per campaign.
- Set the date permission must be in hand, and what happens if a campus or a national says no.
- Name the one guest action: a sample, a sign-up, or a photo the brand may reuse.
- State whether next term is inside this contract or a new approval.

Have each agency mark every row included, excluded, or unknown. The agency can commit to the organizations, the dates, the roles, and your definitions.
