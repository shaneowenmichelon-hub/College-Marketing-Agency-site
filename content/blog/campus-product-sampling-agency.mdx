A campus product sampling agency is the partner a brand hires to put a product in students' hands on named campuses, with a crew, a permission, and a count of what was actually handed out. The pages that already explain formats are the [on-campus product sampling guide](/insights/on-campus-product-sampling) and the sampling ideas article. This page is the hiring question those guides leave open: what the contract should cover, what you should refuse, and how Collegiate Agency prices the work.

## What are you buying when you hire a sampling agency?

You are not buying a list of ideas. You are buying a day, or a series of days, when a defined product moves from a case to a student under rules you wrote down. The agency owns the crew, the setup, and the recap. You own the product, the claims, and the decision to stop if a campus says no.

[Product placement](/services/product-placement) is the published campus rate: $2,500 per campus for placement with Greek-life organizations and media deliverables, when that is the job. A staffed sampling hour is an event. [Campus events](/services/events) run from $500 to $500,000 depending on the package. Both numbers are on the [pricing page](/pricing). A proposal that quotes a single "sampling program" fee and will not say which line is the crew has mixed two purchases. Split them before you compare agencies.

A sealed snack, a beverage, and a personal-care item do not share a handling rule. Write the product into the brief. If the sample is food, read the [CDC prevention page on food poisoning](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention/index.html) with the person who owns product safety, and put the temperature and handoff rules in the scope. Collegiate Agency follows the rule you confirm. It does not invent a food-safety approval.

## What should the sampling contract name?

Name the campuses one by one. "College students" is not a campus. Collegiate Agency's published network is 21 schools. A school outside that list is a separate yes or no, the same way any other off-list campus is. Do not assume a sampling win on one school transfers to the next school on the invoice.

Name the sample itself. Unit size, how many units ship, who receives the shipment, and what happens to leftovers. A contract that says "product trial" and never counts units will recap feelings. Ask for the number you will check, such as samples handed to a student who could actually use the product, and say what does not count, such as a unit left on an empty table.

Name the crew. A [brand ambassador program](/services/brand-ambassadors) at $150 per ambassador per month is the managed student layer, including two posts, when you want posts as well as the handoff. If the only job is a staffed hour with no posts, say that, and do not pay for a content package you will not use. If you want both, they are two lines.

Table: What the sampling contract should pin down before a case is shipped

| Line | What to write | What a missing line causes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Campus and date | School, day, and the approval you need | A crew with nowhere to stand |
| Product | Unit, count, storage, leftovers | A recap with no number you can check |
| Crew | Who hands the sample out, and who does not | Your team recruiting on the day |
| Count | What counts as a sample, and what does not | Clicks reported in place of trial |
| Stop rule | What happens if the campus says no | A fee with no date and no refund |

Use the table as the agenda of the pitch, not as a slogan. Have each agency mark every row included, excluded, or unknown. Collegiate Agency will do that on the schools it can staff. It will not paste another brand's trial number into your forecast.

## Which claims and which people stay out of the caption?

Attendees holding a sample are attendees. A bartender in a venue photo is a bartender. Neither is your staff, and neither is a result. The recap can say how many units left the table. It cannot say those students now prefer the brand unless you measured that and can show the method. Collegiate Agency will not write that sentence for you.

If the people who hand out samples also post, the post has to show the connection when a viewer would not expect it. Put the disclosure in the brief next to the claim language your counsel approved. A sampling hour and a paid post are easy to blur in a caption. Keep them in separate sentences in the contract so the recap can too.

## How does this sit next to the sampling guides already on the site?

Read the format guides when you are choosing what kind of hour to run. Read this page when you are choosing who runs it and what they owe you. Repeating the format list here would compete with those articles and would not help you sign a contract.

Send the same brief to every finalist:

- Campuses, dates, and the approval each campus requires.
- The product, the unit count, the storage rule, and the leftover rule.
- The crew, and whether posts are in scope.
- The count that defines a sample, and the count that does not.
- The disclosure and the claims your counsel has marked.
- The fee split between the event and any monthly ambassador line.
- The stop rule if a campus refuses the date.

A blank row is a reason to wait, not a reason to hope the agency will sort it out on the yard. Collegiate Agency replies within one business day with a tailored plan and firm pricing after you send that sheet. The reply quotes the published rates first. Anything outside them is a campaign quote, not a surprise line after the product has shipped.

## What should you refuse, and when do you write?

Refuse a pitch that says sampling and never names a school. Refuse a per-campus fee that still asks your warehouse to recruit the crew. Refuse a recap template that has a space for impressions and no space for units handed out. Refuse a photograph that the agency cannot place on a campus and a date.
