A campus pop-up activation is a short brand build on a campus, or on ground the campus controls, for a set hour, with permission to stand there and a plan to leave. It is not a content series and it is not a permanent shop. Collegiate Agency runs that date as a campus event, and this page covers the permission, the footprint, the crew, and the hour the build comes down.

## What has to be true before a pop-up is a plan?

Name the ground. A quad, a student-center lobby, a venue lot, and a city sidewalk in front of campus are four different approvers. Write the one you mean. Write who signs, and the date the signature has to be in hand. If that date passes with no signature, the freight does not ship.

One public example of that kind of rule is the [University of Florida regulation on commercial activity](https://policy.ufl.edu/regulation/4-006/). It requires express written permission before someone enters that campus for commercial activity, which the regulation describes as an act or event that results in financial gain. A registered student organization that sponsors an off-campus vendor also needs an event permit for that date. That is one university's text. It is not a national permit. Another school, a city, or a landlord can ask for something else. Collegiate Agency still expects the brief to name the document for the ground you picked.

[Campus events](/services/events) are the service that staffs the date, from a small activation to a presenting package, priced from $500 to $500,000. If the pop-up also puts product into a student organization's hands under the placement offer, that line is [product placement](/services/product-placement) at $2,500 per campus. Do not hide the placement inside the build fee. The [pricing page](/pricing) is the rate card. A pop-up the card does not cover is quoted per campaign.

The cart in that photograph is a giveaway layout from an activation Collegiate Agency ran. It is a useful picture of a small footprint. It is not permission to roll the same cart onto a quad. The footprint in your brief should say how wide the build is, where the line forms, where power comes from, and which path has to stay open. A build that blocks a door or a fire lane is not a creative choice. It is a reason for the campus to shut the date down.

## What does the crew do, and what do the guests stay?

The crew builds, staffs, and strikes. Guests try the product, take a photo, or walk past. Those are different people. Do not write the recap so that everyone in a picture becomes staff. The step-and-repeat frame on this page shows two attendees holding BEATBOX cartons. The artwork behind them is a film's own backdrop. The film, its studio, and its cast are not Collegiate Agency clients, and the photo is not a claim that they are.

If the pop-up includes sampling, use the [campus product sampling agency guide](/insights/campus-product-sampling-agency) for the unit count and the handling rule. This page does not restate that contract. If the pop-up is only a photo moment with no sample, say so, and do not pay for a sample line.

The permission row does not get easier because the build is small. A one-hour cart can need the same signature as a larger tent. The difference is the strike. A pop-up that is still on the quad the next morning was not a pop-up. Put the strike time in the contract and say who confirms the ground is clear.

## What should the quote and the recap contain?

Ask for the campus, the approver, the open, the strike, the crew size, the product if any, and the count you will check. A count can be samples handed out, conversations you defined in advance, or posts the crew was assigned. Pick one. Do not accept "engagement" as the count. You cannot audit a word that the agency gets to define after the day.

Collegiate Agency replies within one business day with a tailored plan and firm pricing after you send the ground and the date. The plan should say whether the date is inside the published event range or is a custom quote. It should also say what you get back if the campus refuses. A refused pop-up is not an invitation to keep the fee and email a gallery from a different school.

Use photographs that match the date you bought. The pair in that frame are attendees at a venue event. They are not a pop-up crew, and the caption should not call them one. When the recap arrives, match each photo to the campus and the hour. If the agency cannot, the photo is not proof.

## What do you send, and what do you turn down?

Turn down a proposal that uses the words pop-up and never names the ground. Turn down a build drawing with no strike time. Turn down a budget that asks your interns to staff the hour the agency priced. Turn down a case study photo that the agency will not locate.

Send this sheet:

- The ground, the approver, and the permission deadline.
- The open, the strike, and who confirms the site is clear.
- The footprint, the power, and the path that stays open.
- The product or the offer, and the one count you will check.
- The crew, and the work your team will not do.
- The stop rule if permission is late.

When the sheet is filled, [contact Collegiate Agency](/contact) with the campus and the week. A pop-up is a short date. The brief should be short too, and it should still be specific enough that a person who was not in the pitch can tell whether the day happened.
