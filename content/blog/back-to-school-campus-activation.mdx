A back-to-school campus activation is a brand date during move-in or the first weeks of class, on ground a campus has agreed to let you use. The crowd is not the permission. Collegiate Agency staffs the date when the approval is real.

The short-build field plan is the [campus pop-up activation guide](/insights/campus-pop-up-activation). This page is the calendar: when to ask, what move-in will not tolerate, and what the recap may claim. Read the pop-up guide for the footprint. Read this page for the week.

## When do you have to start?

Start the term before. Residence life publishes a move-in map, a volunteer call, and a list of what may not block a door. If you are not in that conversation before the map is finished, you are late. A brand that emails in the same week students fly in is competing with families, carts, and the university's own tents. Collegiate Agency replies within one business day once you send a brief. That reply cannot create a permission the campus has already closed.

[Campus events](/services/events) are the staffed hour, from $500 to $500,000. Welcome week can be a small table or a larger welcome event. The price follows the footprint and the crew, not the fact that the week has a nickname. [Product placement](/services/product-placement) at $2,500 per campus is the line for product that goes to an approved organization rather than to a random hand on the sidewalk. Both rates are on the [pricing page](/pricing). A "back-to-school package" that hides those lines is not a package. It is a lump you cannot cut when one school says no.

## What permission still applies when the campus is already full of brands?

Move-in does not suspend the rules. The [University of Florida regulation on commercial activity](https://policy.ufl.edu/regulation/4-006/) requires express written permission before someone enters that campus for commercial activity, described there as an act or event that results in financial gain. A student organization sponsoring an off-campus vendor needs an event permit for that date. That text is one university. It shows the category of document you should expect: written permission, a host, and a date. Another school will name a different office. Collegiate Agency will not treat a busy sidewalk as a yes.

Ask what may be handed out. Some campuses limit food, flyers, or anything that competes with an official welcome bag. The [welcome bag inserts guide](/insights/college-welcome-bag-inserts) is the buying page for that format. If your item belongs in the official bag, buy the bag. Do not also set up a table that hands out a rival item the campus did not approve. If the bag is closed, a table is a different purchase, with its own spot on the map.

A stage and a crowd are a night, not a move-in morning. Families unloading cars are not that audience. Do not plan the activation as a show and then be surprised the campus offered you a folding table by a sidewalk. Take the table if it reaches the students you came for. Do not demand the show.

Table: Move-in week versus a later campus date

| Question | Move-in week | A date after classes settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who else is on the ground | Families, volunteers, university tents | Whoever else booked that day |
| What the campus protects | Doors, carts, check-in lines | The ordinary rules for that space |
| What a brand can usually fit | A small, fast handoff | A wider range of builds |
| What the recap must not claim | That every student who moved in saw you | That the date was move-in |

The last row is the one brands miss. Enrollment is not impressions. A university's move-in count is not your sample count. Collegiate Agency will report the samples, the posts, or the conversations you defined. It will not report the size of the freshman class as if those students walked past your table.

## What should the footprint respect?

Leave the check-in line alone. Leave the elevator and the residence-hall door alone. A photo in a doorway is not worth the student who cannot get home. Write the footprint in feet, the side of the path you will use, and the strike time, which should be before the campus needs the ground for its own evening program.

That night photo is not a sunrise move-in. Use night frames for night dates. For move-in, shoot the table you were given, in the light you were given, and label the campus. The people who take a sample are attendees or family, not staff, unless they are on your crew list. Collegiate Agency's published network is 21 campuses. A school outside it can be in the plan only after a separate yes. Do not print a national move-in map that includes schools you cannot staff.

## What do you send the term before?

Send the sheet while the campus can still say yes:

- The schools and the move-in dates, from each school's calendar.
- The ground you want, and the ground you will accept if the first is taken.
- The item, and whether it belongs in an official bag or on a table.
- The footprint, the path you will not block, and the strike time.
- The count you will check, which is not the enrollment number.
- The creative deadline, which has to fall before the permission deadline.

Expect the campus to care more about traffic flow than about your creative. A beautiful tent in the wrong place is a no. A plain table in the right place is a yes. Collegiate Agency will take the plain table. The photographs will look like move-in, which is the point. They will show carts, families, and a small setup, not a nightclub. If that is not the picture the brand wants, buy a different week and say so now.

Put a person on the ground who can make a small decision. If the campus moves you ten feet, the crew should move ten feet without a conference call. Write that authority into the brief. A supervisor who is only reachable by email will lose the spot while they wait. Collegiate Agency staffs the hour with someone who can take that instruction from the campus and still hit the count you defined.

Hold the creative until the school list is real. Printing twenty campus names when twelve are confirmed wastes the print and misleads the student who sees a school you will not visit. Version the art. Ship the confirmed list. Add a school only after its permission is in the same folder as the others. That discipline is the whole back-to-school plan: fewer schools, earlier asks, and a recap that matches the map the campus drew before you arrived.
