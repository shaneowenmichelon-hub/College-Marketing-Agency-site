A multi-campus brand activation is one offer, run on more than one school you named, with a permission and a recap for each school. The poster can be the same. The approval cannot.

Collegiate Agency staffs this as a set of campus events, not as a single national hour. If you want a short test before you buy the longer list, use the [campus field marketing agency guide](/insights/campus-field-marketing-agency). This page is the brief for the list itself. A list without a per-campus permission is a poster, not an activation.

## What can be shared, and what has to be written per campus?

Share the product, the sentence you are allowed to say, the disclosure, and the definition of a result. Those should not drift between the first school and the last, or you will not be able to compare them. Do not share the permission. A student organization that said yes in one city has not said yes in the next city. A facility office that approved a 10-by-10 tent has not approved that tent on a different quad.

[Campus events](/services/events) are priced from $500 to $500,000 per the package you buy, and the price is not a bulk discount you should invent by stacking schools into one unnamed fee. A [brand ambassador program](/services/brand-ambassadors) at $150 per ambassador per month can sit under the dates when you want posts between them. Those rates are on the [pricing page](/pricing). Ask the quote to show a line per campus for the event, and a line for any monthly program, so a refused school can come off the invoice without a fight about what the fee included.

Those two people are attendees at one event. They are not a national sample, and they are not proof that the same hour ran on a second campus. When the recap has ten schools, each photo needs a campus and a date. A grid of similar frames without labels is not a multi-campus report. It is a folder.

## How do you keep the count comparable?

Pick one count and use it everywhere. Samples handed to a student, posts published by the crew, or a sign-in you designed are three counts. Pick one before the first load-in. If a campus will not allow that count, write the exception on that campus's line. Do not quietly switch the whole program to a softer number so the chart still goes up.

If the form asks for a name, an email, or a phone number, read the [NIST guide to protecting personally identifiable information](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/122/final) with the person who owns your data review. The guide is about collecting only what you need and limiting how long you keep it. It is not a form you can paste into a terms box and call finished. Collegiate Agency will use the fields you approved and will not keep the list for a later brand unless the brief says that in writing. A shared tablet on five campuses is still five collections under one rule.

Table: What stays the same across campuses, and what is rewritten

| Item | Same on every campus | Written per campus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offer and claims | Yes, unless counsel marks an exception | The exception, if any |
| Permission | No | Approver, date, and what was approved |
| Crew | The role, not the person | Who is on site that day |
| Count | The definition | The number, and any campus that refused it |
| Photos | The labeling rule | The frames from that day |

A row that says "same" in the last column is a mistake. Permission and photos are never national. The offer can be. Holding that line is the whole point of buying one partner instead of five local vendors who each rewrite the claim.

## What does the route look like in practice?

Order the schools by the permission you can actually get, not by the prestige of the name. A famous campus that has not answered is not the first stop. Collegiate Agency's published network lists 21 campuses. A school off that list can still be in the brief, and the answer may be that it cannot be staffed on the week you want. Put that answer next to the school before you print creative with its name on it.

Ship one kit description to every stop: what is in the case, what has to come home, and what can be left if the campus requires it. A kit that changes in the parking lot will show up as a different offer in the photos. The recap should say if a school ran a smaller setup, and why. Readers of the report should not have to guess which tent was the real program.

Hats on attendees show that a giveaway happened at that stop. They do not show that the same giveaway happened at the other schools on the route. Label the photo with the campus. Do not caption the wearers as staff or as reps. They are guests.

## What should the partner owe you at the end?

A one-page rollup can sit on top. Under it, one block per campus: approval, date, crew, count, exceptions, and photos. A rollup with no blocks is a national number you cannot audit. Collegiate Agency will not add the schools together into a single reach figure it did not define with you in the brief.

Refuse a proposal that prices "multi-campus" as one lump with no school names. Refuse a creative deck that lists twenty schools and a staffing plan that lists three. Refuse a data file that arrives with columns you did not approve.

The sheet itself is short:

- The offer, the claim, and the disclosure, written once.
- Every campus, with its approver and the date you need the yes.
- The count, and any campus that cannot use that count.
- The kit, and what has to come home.
- The fields you will collect, if you collect any, and how long you will keep them.

Send the offer, the school list, the count, and the week you need answers on permission. [Contact Collegiate Agency](/contact) with that page. The reply, within one business day, marks each school as staffable on your dates, staffable later, or not in scope. That markup is the brief. The truck should not leave before it exists.

Build the route in the order permission comes back, and leave a spare week between the last yes and the first load-in. A Friday yes does not make a Saturday tent reasonable on a campus that still has to reserve the ground. Collegiate Agency would rather drop a school from the first wave than pretend the approval arrived. You can add that school to a second wave when the paper is real. The creative does not have to be reprinted for a school that moves, as long as the name on the tent matches the campus that said yes.

Compare the stops only on the count you defined. A school that handed out 400 samples and a school that published 12 posts are not ahead of or behind each other. They did different jobs. If you need one chart, make one chart per count, and keep the school names on the rows. A single bar for "the tour" is how a weak stop hides inside a strong one. The point of hiring one partner for many campuses is that you can see the stops side by side, not that you can collapse them.
