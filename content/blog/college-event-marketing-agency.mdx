A college event marketing agency is the partner a brand hires to put a crew on a named campus date, with permission to be there and a count you can check after the hour. It is not the same purchase as a logo on a student organization's banner. Collegiate Agency staffs campus events, and this page is the hiring choice between producing a date and buying into someone else's night.

The sponsorship buying guide is separate, on the [college event sponsorship page](/insights/college-event-sponsorship). Use that page when you are buying into someone else's night. Use this page when you are hiring the team that runs a date. A brand that needs both can buy both, as two lines, with two recaps. Combining them into one adjective is how the crew disappears from the invoice while the fee stays.

## What is included in the event, and what is only a logo?

Write the role before you write the check. A staffed sampling hour, a hosted tent, a tour stop, and a paid mention in a program are four different jobs. The first three need people. The fourth needs a contract with the organizer and may not need your agency on site at all. If a proposal uses one price for all four, split it.

[Campus events](/services/events) are the staffed work, priced from $500 to $500,000. A [brand ambassador program](/services/brand-ambassadors) at $150 per ambassador per month is the ongoing student layer, including two posts, when the event is part of a longer program rather than one night. Both rates are on the [pricing page](/pricing). Collegiate Agency quotes those first. A date the card does not cover is a campaign quote, with the campus and the hour written on it.

The people in that photograph are attendees. They are not the crew, and a college event quote should not describe them as the team you are hiring. The crew is the group on the schedule. Guests are the people the crew is there to meet. The recap can count guests only in the way you defined before doors, such as samples handed out or a sign-in you actually used. It cannot turn a crowded photo into a headcount.

## What has to be settled with the campus?

The campus, the venue, or the student organization controls the ground. Name which one signs, and the day the signature is due. A public university also has access duties of its own. The U.S. Department of Justice publishes the [ADA Title II regulations](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-ii-2010-regulations/). Title II applies to public entities, which includes a public university. Ask the campus which access rules apply to a brand setup on its property: the route in, the line, the height of a counter, the restroom path, and the way a guest gets the same offer if they cannot stand in that line. Put the answer in the brief. Collegiate Agency builds to the rules you and the campus confirm. It does not give legal advice, and it does not treat a crowd photo as an access plan. A private campus can have its own rules that are not Title II. Ask there too.

Read the middle column before you negotiate price. An agency that is only introducing you to an organizer is not producing the hour. Pay that introduction, if you want it, as an introduction. Do not pay it as production and then discover your team is staffing the tent.

## What does a usable recap look like?

Ask for the campus, the date, the approval, the crew names or the crew count, the access notes you required, and the one number you defined. Photographs should be from that night. A frame from another city proves that other city.

That can-in-hand frame is a sampling moment. Use it in a recap only when the date you bought was that kind of hour, on that campus. If the event was a panel or a game-day tent with no product in hand, do not illustrate the recap with a drink photo and call it the same job. Collegiate Agency will label the campus and the date. It will not borrow a result from a different activation.

Weather, a fire alarm, or a campus shutdown can end the hour early. The contract should say what happens to the fee. A full fee for a date that never opened is not a plan. A written rule, agreed before load-in, is a plan.

## What do you send, and what do you turn away?

Turn away a deck that could be sent to any college in the country without changing a proper noun. Turn away a reach number with no method. Turn away a scope that says the agency will "support" the event and then lists tasks your coordinator still has to do.

Send this:

- The campus, the date, and the approver.
- The role you are buying, in one sentence.
- The crew, and the tasks your team will not do.
- The access rules the campus already gave you, and the ones still open.
- The one count, and the photos you expect from that night.
- The stop rule if the date is refused or cut short.

[Contact Collegiate Agency](/contact) when that list is filled. The reply, within one business day, is a tailored plan and firm pricing, including a direct no if the campus is outside the published network of 21 and cannot be staffed in time. A college event is a date. The proposal should read like a date, not like a slogan.

If you are comparing three agencies, send all three the same sheet and score the replies on the blanks, not on the design of the deck. The agency that names the approver, the crew, and the count is ahead of the agency that names a feeling. Collegiate Agency will leave a row blank when it does not know the answer yet. A filled row that invents an approver is worse than a blank one. Ask who the agency called, and when, before you treat a campus name in a headline as a campus that has said yes.

Keep the student-organization relationship clean. If a student group is the host, the group is not your subsidiary and its members are not your employees for the hour unless the contract says that and the campus allows it. Pay the group what the sponsorship or the hosting fee actually is, on its own line. Pay the agency for the crew on a different line. A student who shows up as a guest should stay a guest in the recap, the caption, and the thank-you post. That distinction is how the date stays honest when the photos are the only thing a person who was not there will see.
