A student housing marketing agency is the outside partner an owner, operator, developer, or property marketing director hires to fill beds before move-in. The pages that rank for this search sell search, paid media, websites, and branding. Collegiate Agency fills the layer those pages leave open: managed on-campus execution, on-site activations, campus events, peer posts, and localized ads that reuse the same creative.

## What does a student housing marketing agency actually sell?

Two offers share this name. One is a digital system that lives in an ad account and on a website. The other is a crew on the campus where the beds are. You can buy both. You should not buy them as one unnamed fee.

GTMA's article [Student Housing Marketing: How to Turn Search Into Signed Leases](https://gtma.agency/blog/student-housing-marketing-how-to-turn-search-into-signed-leases/) (GTMA, August 17, 2026) opens with a direct answer written for search and AI engines. It says the best way to market student housing is to answer the questions students and parents ask before a tour. The tools it names are SEO, web design, paid media, social media, virtual tours, photography, video, reputation marketing, and analytics. The same article then describes the season as a sequence of searches. A student searching in October may be planning for next fall. A parent searching in February may be comparing safety, distance, total cost, and lease terms. A roommate group searching in April may need a bed count and an individual lease before another community fills. Later, the article defines a complete system as a mobile website, local search, paid search and social ads, brand creative, organic social, email, and dashboards. That is a clear digital scope. It does not name a staffed campus date, a crew, or a post that a student on that campus publishes.

A campus-execution partner sells the missing layer. [Campus events](/services/events) put the property into a room, a quad, or a show students already planned to attend. Peer posts carry one leasing offer for that school. Localized ads then repeat the same flyer or video to students near that campus. Collegiate Agency sells that layer. It does not replace the floor-plan pages, the tour form, or the search program your digital vendor already runs.

Keep the vendors on separate lines. If a proposal says it covers search, the site, and a staffed week, ask which line is media and which line is a person on campus. Your leasing office still owns tours, applications, and the lease file. The campus partner owns the dates, the posts, and the ad result you defined before the first post went live.

## How does a digital-only agency differ from campus execution?

Score the proposal against the work, not the category name. A firm can be excellent at search and still have nobody who can stand on your campus this month. A leasing team can be excellent at tours and still have no one building the date.

Table: What a digital-only student housing agency, a campus-execution agency, and an in-house leasing team each cover

| Partner | What they run | What stays with your team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digital-only student housing agency | Search, the website, paid media, email, and brand creative | Tours, applications, and any on-campus date |
| Campus-execution agency | Staffed activations, campus events, peer posts, and local ads of that same creative | The lease file, and the digital vendor if you keep one |
| In-house leasing team | Day-to-day tours, renewals, and the property's own accounts | A campus crew, unless you hire and brief one |

Read the rows before you compare price. If the only deliverables are rankings, a new site, and an ad account, you are buying the first row. If the proposal names campuses, dates, a crew, a post count, and the ad that reuses the post, you are buying the second row. If the work sits on your community managers and leasing agents, you are buying the third row, and the open question is who builds the campus date.

Ask each finalist for the schools it can staff, the date of the first activation, and the recap fields you will receive. Collegiate Agency quotes the campus layer against the rates on the [pricing page](/pricing). It will not paste another community's occupancy into your forecast. A number from a different property is not your plan.

## What belongs in a 2027-28 pre-leasing brief?

For a property leasing the 2027-28 academic year, use the October-to-April sequence in the GTMA article above. The brief can go out now, while an October searcher may be planning for next fall, and it can stay active through the April roommate push. Collegiate Agency does not publish a national pre-leasing calendar. Your property's own pace sets the weeks you buy. Do not treat a semester label as a schedule.

Name the schools, the beds still open, and the one action you want. A tour, an application start, or a saved floor plan are three different asks. Pick one for this contract. Name the date permission for an on-campus activation must be in hand, and what happens to the fee if a campus says no. Say whether parents are the audience for the website and the email, and whether students are the audience for the campus date. Those are different messages. Do not ask one caption to do both.

Housing ads need a review by your counsel before they run. Ask which limits apply to age, sex, and location targeting, and which words an ad for a dwelling may not use. Put that answer in the brief so the peer posts and the localized ads follow the same rule. Collegiate Agency runs the handoff you approve. It does not replace that review.

Send every finalist the same sheet:

- Campuses, open beds, and the academic year on the lease.
- The one guest action, and how you will count it.
- Whether the digital vendor stays, and who owns the ad account.
- The on-campus dates, the crew, and what a missed date does to the fee.
- The creative that peer posts and local ads must share.
- The housing-ad limits your counsel has already marked.

A blank cell is a reason to wait, not a reason to sign. Have each agency mark every row included, excluded, or unknown. The agency can commit to the schools, the dates, the posts, and your definitions. It cannot commit to a lease your office has not written.

## How should on-campus work show up in a lease-up?

The campus layer is managed execution. The [brand ambassador program](/services/brand-ambassadors) is the service page for that scope: screened students, a monthly rate, and posts you can audit. This page does not restate how to recruit or run that program. If you are buying a field pilot with local ownership and a recap you can compare, use the [campus field marketing agency guide](/insights/campus-field-marketing-agency). If the buy is a staffed activation with a date and proof of what happened, use the [campus brand activation agency guide](/insights/campus-brand-activation-agency). A lease-up brief uses those jobs as pieces, then adds the property's leasing calendar.

The leasing example Collegiate Agency has published is the [SOS Consultants Nola case study](/work/sos-consultants-nola-campus-leasing-ambassadors). The package was $2,500. It covered two shows, an on-site activation at the show, and a round of ambassador posts. The posts used dedicated flyer and video creative, and that same creative ran as localized paid advertising on Meta. From that $2,500 package spend, the case study reports an 11x return on ad spend. Those are the figures on the page. They are not a forecast for your community, and they are not the current rate card.

Use the shape of that package, not the headline, when you write the scope. One offer. The same flyer and video in the peer posts and in the local ads. A live date students already planned to attend. A recap that lists the shows, the posts, and the ad result you defined. If a proposal uses a different creative in the ads than in the posts, it has split the campaign that case study kept as one system.

## What should the contract price, and what should you refuse?

Price the published rates first. A brand ambassador program is $150 per ambassador per month, including 2 dedicated social posts with links and brand tags. Localized paid media is an add-on on that rate. Campus event packages run from $500 to $500,000. A brief those rates do not cover is quoted per campaign. The $2,500 figure above is the published case-study package. It is not the monthly ambassador rate, and it is not a per-campus product-placement rate.

Refuse a proposal that does any of these:

- Names student housing and never names a campus or a date.
- Treats a website redesign as the whole lease-up plan.
- Charges an execution fee and still asks your team to recruit the crew.
- Reports clicks and will not define a tour, an application, or a post.
- Uses different creative in the ads than in the peer posts.
- Copies another property's occupancy into your forecast.
