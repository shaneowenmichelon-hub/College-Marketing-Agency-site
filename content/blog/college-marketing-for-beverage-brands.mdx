## What has to be true for a cold can on campus?

A cold can is not a sampling hour with ice added at the last minute. Name the count you will ship, who signs for it, where it sits overnight, and the temperature your product requires. If the campus will not allow that storage, do not put the product on the table.

Campus dining, a student union, and a stadium can each already name a beverage company. A sampling table and a concession pour are different permissions. Collegiate Agency will not promise a pour the campus has not approved in writing. Get that answer from the office that controls the door. Do not copy another school's contract into the brief.

The recap uses the same five words as the table: shipped, received, handed out, wasted, and returned. A toast can show cans in hand. It cannot replace the count. Collegiate Agency will not fill a missing case with another brand's campus total.

If the product is an energy drink or another high-caffeine drink, ask whether the campus wants a separate approval or a limit on what a student may say. A student may not add a focus claim, a workout claim, or a health claim you did not approve.

## What changes when the drink is alcoholic or non-alcoholic?

Name the age check before you book the hour. If the drink contains alcohol, the sample is for people 21 and older, and the campus alcohol policy still applies. If the drink does not contain alcohol, ask whether the campus treats a beer lookalike, a spirits lookalike, or a ready-to-drink can under that same policy. A non-alcoholic label is not a pass.

## How should a paid student post disclose the relationship?

The Federal Trade Commission adopted revised [Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm) on July 26, 2023. A payment, a free or discounted product, or the chance of a prize is a material connection. When a significant minority of the audience would not expect that connection, it has to be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, which the guides define as difficult to miss and easy to understand. In social media, the disclosure should be unavoidable. A note that sits only on a profile page is easy to miss. The guides also describe a college student who earns points for posts and can trade those points for prizes such as concert tickets or electronics. Those incentives have to be disclosed, and the advertiser should check that the disclosure is on the post.

Put one sentence in the brief, in the language of the post, and say where it sits. Collegiate Agency checks the live post against that sentence. A free can and a paid month are both connections. Your counsel owns the wording.

Use the same code as the table, or a second code you can tell apart. A shared code with no campus on it cannot tell you which school moved. Name the page the QR code opens. A store locator is a door. A homepage with no store on it is a visit.

## What should a beverage proposal refuse?

Refuse one national price. Refuse a reach number with no campus on it. Refuse a trial rate copied from another brand's can count, a pour the dining contract does not allow, and a student club billed at $1,500 per sorority (chapter) when it is not a sorority (chapter).
