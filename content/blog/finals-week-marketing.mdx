Finals week marketing is a brand offer that has to fit an exam schedule, on a campus that is trying to get students through tests, not through a line for a sample. It is a bad week for a loud activation and a possible week for a quiet one the campus actually wants. Collegiate Agency will staff the quiet version when the school says yes.

It will not rename a festival as a study break. If the idea needs a speaker, a line, or a blocked door, move it to a week when the registrar is not running the campus. The students are not an audience that week. They are in the middle of a test.

## What is the campus doing that week?

Students are in exams. Libraries, unions, and late-night rooms are full. A registrar, not a brand, sets the clock. Georgia Tech's registrar publishes [final exam guidelines](https://registrar.gatech.edu/registration/exams) that put exams at the time in the Final Exam Matrix. The page says no exam should be given at another time without the registrar's prior approval, and that exceptions should be in the syllabus. Departments have to ask academic scheduling for a room if the exam is outside the official period. That is one university's rule. It is not your brand's rule, and it is not every school's rule. It is a clear picture of the constraint: the hour you want may already be an exam.

Read the calendar of the campus you named before you propose a date. If your hour overlaps a block when thousands of students are in classrooms, do not plan a build outside the door they will pour through. You will be in the way. [Campus events](/services/events) can still be the right service if the campus offers a late window, a union corner, or a residence-hall lobby that is open for a quiet handoff. Packages run from $500 to $500,000. A finals date is usually the small end of that range. Do not buy the large end and then ask the school to tolerate it.

## What should you run, and what should you skip?

Run something a student can take while walking to the library: a sealed snack that your safety review allows, a pen, a card with one offer, a short post that does not ask them to leave the building. Skip a DJ, a sampling theater, a photo booth that needs a line, and any build that blocks a corridor. Product in a study kit can be [product placement](/services/product-placement) at $2,500 per campus when that offer fits, which is a case in an approved spot, not a table in a stairwell. The third row is a no. A brand that needs a crowd should buy a different week. The [campus pop-up activation guide](/insights/campus-pop-up-activation) is the field plan for a short build when the campus is not in exams. Finals is not the week to test that plan.

## What does the brief say about noise, rooms, and claims?

Write the decibel expectation in plain language: conversation, not music. Write the room. Write the strike time, which should be before the next exam block if you are anywhere near a classroom. Write the product rule. A caffeinated sample still needs your safety review and the campus rule on food. Do not claim the product improves grades, focus, or sleep. You do not have that proof, and Collegiate Agency will not print it.

The step-and-repeat is a premiere, not a library. The film, the studio, and the cast are not Collegiate Agency clients. The frame does not belong in a finals recap. It belongs in this article as a reminder of the weeks that are built for a backdrop, which finals is not.

If creators post during exams, keep the disclosure a viewer can see, and do not ask them to leave an exam to film. A post from a desk is enough. A post that fakes a party during quiet hours will get the program uninvited.

## What do you send, and what do you postpone?

Send this, or postpone:

- The campus and the exam dates, from that school's calendar.
- The room or the outdoor spot, and who approved it.
- The offer, the noise limit, and the strike time.
- The product rule, with no grade or health claim.
- The count you will check, which should be small and real.
- The reason this cannot wait until the first week back.

Finals week marketing works when the brand is useful and quiet. It fails when the brand is the loudest thing on a campus that asked for silence.

Do not buy a "stress" theme. Students do not need a brand to narrate their exams, and a joke about all-nighters is not a strategy. Offer the item, say what it is, and leave. If the campus asks you to move because a room became an overflow exam site, move. The contract should treat that request as ordinary, not as a breach you will argue about while students are walking into a test. Collegiate Agency will reschedule or stop. It will not insist on the original doorway.

Keep the crew small. Two people and a folding table are easier to place than a six-person build, and they are easier to remove when the hour ends. The count you check should match that scale. A few hundred samples can be a real result in a union lobby. A promise of the whole student body is not. Write the smaller number. Then hit it, label the photos with the campus, and go home before the next exam starts.
