A college app marketing agency is the partner a brand hires to get students to install an app or open an account on campuses the brand names. The hire is a link or a code the brand already tracks, a brief for the posts, and a check that paid posts say so. Collegiate Agency runs that work as campus ambassadors and, when you also want a staffed hour, as a campus event.

The field plan for a short build is the [campus pop-up activation guide](/insights/campus-pop-up-activation). The contract for a student post is the [college creator marketing agency guide](/insights/college-creator-marketing-agency). This page is the app-growth hire: which published rate buys which line, what the brief has to name, and what a proposal should refuse. Read those guides for the footprint and the post. Read this page before you sign for student installs or sign-ups.

## What are you hiring, and what are you leaving out?

You are hiring a way for a student on a named campus to reach an action you defined. Name the action. It might be a store install, a new account, or a first use of the product. You are not hiring a promise that students will like the app. You are not hiring a cost figure per install. Collegiate Agency does not publish one, and it will not paste another brand's download count into your forecast.

The published network is 2,000 student ambassadors on 21 campuses. A school outside that list needs its own yes. Collegiate Agency is based in New York and replies within one business day with a tailored plan and firm pricing once you send a brief. An email, an account, and a funded wallet are different briefs. Say which one the recap will count.

The [brand ambassador program](/services/brand-ambassadors) is $150 per ambassador per month. The rate includes two dedicated posts, flyers or videos, with links and brand tags. Localized paid media is an add-on, not a silent part of that rate. [Campus events](/services/events) are a separate line, from $500 to $500,000, when the launch needs a table, a crew, and an hour. Both rates are on the [pricing page](/pricing). Product placement is $2,500 per campus, for Greek-life organizations and the media in that rate. It is not an app-install package. A proposal that says campus app launch and shows one number has hidden a line. Ask which line.

The hero photograph shows two attendees holding NUTRL cans at a sponsored campus show Collegiate Agency photographed. They are guests. The cans are a beverage, not an app, and the room is not a download report. Do not treat the frame as proof that an app was installed.

## Which line buys a post, and which line buys a staffed hour?

A post can carry a link and still produce zero installs. A table can produce a busy hour and still produce zero accounts if the next screen fails. Buy the line that matches the action you will count. Do not buy a mood and hope the recap invents the rest.

Table: What each published line includes when the product is an app

| Purchase | What you get | What you do not get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambassador month | Two posts with your link or code, on campuses you name | A staffed table, unless you also buy an event |
| Campus event | A crew, a date, and a count of what happened that hour | A month of posts, unless you also buy them |
| Product placement | Product with approved Greek-life organizations, plus that rate's media | An install system, or a download forecast |

The link or the code has to be one you can already read in your own system. Collegiate Agency will put that link in the post and can ask the student to show the live file. It does not replace your analytics. If you need a different parameter per campus, send that parameter in the brief. A shared link with no campus on it cannot later tell you which school moved.

## What do you send before anyone shares the app?

Send one sheet. The reply, within one business day, is a tailored plan and firm pricing against the published rates. The site also publishes 7 days from a signed statement of work to boots on the ground. That clock starts when the statement of work is signed, not when you send the first note. A campus date still needs the school's permission. Collegiate Agency does not publish a fixed number of weeks from the reply to event day.

Put this on the sheet:

- Campuses, and whether each one is inside the published network of 21.
- The action you will count: install, account, or a later step.
- The link or code, one per campus if you need to tell schools apart.
- The claims a student may say, and the sentences that are off limits.
- The disclosure sentence, and where it has to sit in the post.
- Whether a staffed hour is a separate line.
- Who reviews the live post, and how fast that review has to answer.

A growth lead can use the same sheet for a game, a study tool, a banking app, or a consumer subscription. The category does not change the rate. A fintech sign-up that asks a student to connect a bank account is a different brief from a game install. Collegiate Agency will not invent a claim your counsel has not approved.

[Contact Collegiate Agency](/contact) with the schools and the month you want the posts to run. You can also [build a campaign](/build-a-campaign) and send the same facts through the campaign builder. The builder multiplies the ambassador rate by the count and the months you set, and it adds event fees only for the inventory you select. A school you cannot name yet should stay off the sheet.

Review the live post, not the draft you liked in a deck. A link that was correct during approval and wrong on the live story did not ship. If a post has to come down, the brief should say who tells the student and how fast. Ask for the campus on every row of the recap. A national total with no school beside it cannot be checked against the brief.

## When does a student post have to show it is paid?

If a student is paid, gifted, or collecting points to talk about the app, viewers who would not expect that connection need to see it. The Federal Trade Commission adopted revised [Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm) in the Federal Register on July 26, 2023. Section 255.5 says a connection that might materially affect the weight or credibility of an endorsement, when the audience would not reasonably expect it, must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. The same section says material connections can include payment, free or discounted products, and other benefits such as the possibility of being paid or of winning a prize.

The same Federal Register text includes an example of a college student who earns points for posting about a product and can trade those points for prizes. Those incentives should be disclosed, and the advertiser should take steps to make sure the disclosure is there. Another passage says online disclosures have to be unavoidable, and it describes a disclosure that sits only behind a link labeled "more" as one people can miss. Section 255.1 says advertisers should guide endorsers, monitor them, and remedy misses, and that this is not a safe harbor.

That page is a guide the Commission adopted. It is not a ruling on your post. Put the sentence in the brief, in the language the post will use, and say where it sits. Collegiate Agency checks the live post against that sentence. Your counsel still owns the claim. A referral code is still a connection if the student is paid, gifted, or collecting points to share it. If the post is a short video, the sentence has to be in the video, not only in a caption a viewer can skip.

## What should you refuse before you sign?

Refuse a roster that is only follower counts. Refuse a download number built from another company's campus tour. Refuse a line that says students will download it, with no campus names and no link you can read. Refuse the right to use a student's face in national ads unless the brief says where, how long, and for which brand. Refuse a single price that mixes a month of posts and a staffed launch without showing both lines.
