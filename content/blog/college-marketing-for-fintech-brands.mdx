## What should a fintech brand require before it hires?

Send one sheet before you sign.

- Name the product: a credit card, a debit or checking account, a budgeting app, or an investing app.
- Name the campuses, and whether each one is inside the published network of 21.
- Name the action you will count, and the code your own system already reads.

## What does the credit card rule change on a campus table?

Public Law 111-24, the Credit Card Accountability Responsibility and Disclosure Act of 2009, was signed on May 22, 2009. Section 304 says a card issuer or creditor may not offer a student at an institution of higher education a tangible item to induce that student to apply for or participate in an open end consumer credit plan, when the offer is on campus, near campus, or at an event sponsored by or related to the school. The same section says a school shall publicly disclose a contract with a card issuer or creditor for marketing a credit card. A sense of Congress about debt education at orientation is not a mandate and does not approve a giveaway. Read section 304 in the [Credit CARD Act of 2009](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-111publ24/html/PLAW-111publ24.htm).

The rule now in force is Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.57, on the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau page dated April 8, 2026, [Reporting and marketing rules for college student open-end credit](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/2026-04-08/57/). A card issuer or creditor may not offer a college student a tangible item to induce the student to apply for or open an open-end consumer credit plan on campus, near campus, or at a related event. The official interpretation on that page says a tangible item includes a physical item such as a gift card, a t-shirt, or a magazine subscription, and does not include discounts, rewards points, or promotional credit terms. It treats a spot within 1,000 feet of the campus border, as the school defines that border, as near the campus. A physical item offered whether or not the student applies is not that inducement. The example is refreshments that are not conditioned on an application.

If a card issuer offered that cart only after a college student applied for an open-end credit plan, on campus or near it, counsel has a 1026.57 question. Collegiate Agency will not pack that offer. A budgeting app that does not open credit is a different product. Do not paste the credit-card rule onto it, and do not assume a debit card, a prepaid card, or an overdraft line is outside the rule. The official interpretation excludes an overdraft line accessed by a debit card from the college student credit card definition, and it can include certain hybrid prepaid-credit cards. Ask counsel which product you sell before anyone hands out a shirt. This page is not legal advice.

The school, not the campus crew, discloses a contract made to market a credit card. Do not ask a student to imply the college endorses the card. A welcome week table still needs that school's solicitation or tabling permit. Those rules differ by school.

## What should a non-card app still put in the brief?

A student may say only the sentences your counsel already approved. A student may not add a rate, a fee, a yield, or a comparison you did not write. Collegiate Agency checks the live post against that sheet. It does not invent a sentence when the sheet is blank.

Students do not give account advice. They do not say which account to open, whether to take credit, or what to buy or sell. A financial literacy hour with a student organization needs that organization's written yes, the campus permit, and the same script. Measure the attendance the organization confirms and the completions on that campus's code.

The sign-up stays in your app. The table does not take a Social Security number, a bank login, or a photo of an identity document. Send one code per campus when you need to tell schools apart. Collegiate Agency will put that code in the post. It does not replace your identity check.

## How should a paid student post disclose the relationship?

The Federal Trade Commission adopted revised [Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm) on July 26, 2023. The notice says the guides were effective that day. A payment, a free or discounted product, or the chance of a prize is a material connection when a significant minority of the audience would not expect it. A disclosure must be clear and conspicuous, meaning difficult to miss and easily understood. On social media it should be unavoidable. A note that sits only on a profile page is easy to miss. The guides describe a college student who earns points for posts and can exchange those points for prizes such as concert tickets or electronics. Those incentives have to be disclosed, and the advertiser should check that the disclosure is on the post.

Put one sentence in the brief, in the language of the post, and say where it sits. Collegiate Agency checks the live post against that sentence. Your counsel owns the wording. The content-rights guide covers who may reuse the file. This page does not restate that contract.

## What should a fintech proposal refuse?

Refuse one national price. Refuse a reach number with no campus on it. Refuse a sign-up forecast copied from another company's campus work. Refuse a t-shirt, a gift card, or any other physical item offered to a college student for a credit-card application on or near campus unless your counsel has cleared that offer in writing. Refuse a clipboard that collects a Social Security number or a bank login. Refuse a student club billed at $1,500 per sorority (chapter) when it is not a sorority (chapter).
