## Which college marketing campaign format fits the job?

The hero photograph shows three attendees holding BEATBOX cartons outside a venue event Collegiate Agency photographed. The cartons are product in the frame, not a sample count.

Match the format to the action you will count. If you cannot name the action, you are not ready to pick a format.

Table: How a brand scopes a college marketing campaign by format

| Format | What it is | Best for | What the brand supplies | How it is measured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sampling | A staffed handout of product at a named campus | Trial of a product a student can take that day | The product, the campuses, and the unit you will count | Units handed out at that campus |
| Pop-up or tour stop | A tent, cart, or short install students walk up to | A place, a demo, or a code at a named stop | The build, the offer, and the action at that stop | The action named for that stop |
| Sponsored event | Rights inside an event the brand does not own | A booth, a pour, or a logo on a school calendar event | The rights you want and who owns the event | The rights delivered, plus the count you named |
| Student-organization placement | Product or a fee inside a chapter or club | A group that already meets, when the chapter is the door | The product and which chapters are in scope | Chapters that received it, and posts that went live |
| Paid posts | A month of student posts with a link and a disclosure | A message that has to live after the hour ends | The sentence, the link, and who approves the live post | Live posts that show the disclosure |

A sample is not a pop-up, and a pop-up is not a month of posts. Buy the row that matches the job. Add a second row only when you can name a second action.

Here is an example, not a client result. A beverage brand that needs cans in hands at one school buys the sampling row and names the unit it will count. If that brand also needs posts after the hour, it adds the paid-posts row and names the disclosure sentence. One price that covers both without naming them is the proposal to send back.

## What should you send before a campus campaign starts?

Send one sheet before you sign.

- Name the format from the table, and do not write "a mix" when you mean one row.
- Name the campuses, and whether each one is inside the published network of 21.
- Name the product, the code, or the rights you are buying.
- Name the action you will count, and the system that already records it.
- Name who approves the school's permission and the disclosure sentence.

A student does not post, and a table does not open, until that person has approved the sheet.

The glass is the setup. It is not proof that a campus said yes, and it is not a count of units. Collegiate Agency will not invent the number you want counted.

Permission sits with the school or the event owner, not with the photograph. Ask, in writing, who may approve a table, a tent, or a chapter drop on that campus. A yes for one quad is not a yes for the student union, a dining hall, or a second school. If the answer is no, change the format or the campus. Each proposal line should name one row from the table, one campus, and one count.

## How do a pop-up, a tour stop, and a sponsored event differ?

A pop-up is a place you bring. A tour stop is that place repeated on a route, with the campus name changed on each stop. A sponsored event is someone else's room, and you buy named rights inside it. Do not average tour stops into one national number. If the team also hands out product at an event you do not own, that handout is a second row with its own count.

## How should a paid student post disclose the relationship?

The Federal Trade Commission adopted revised [Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm) on July 26, 2023. The notice says the guides were effective that day. A material connection needs to be disclosed when a significant minority of the audience does not understand or expect it. Clear and conspicuous means difficult to miss and easily understood. On social media the disclosure should be unavoidable. A disclosure that sits only on a profile page is one people can miss. If the post is visible without a "more" tap and the disclosure is not, the guides say that disclosure is not clear and conspicuous. In the material-connection examples, a college student signs up for a program that awards points for posts and can exchange those points for prizes such as concert tickets or electronics. Those incentives have to be disclosed, and the advertiser should take steps to see that the disclosures are on the posts.

A free product for a post is a material connection when viewers would not expect it. Put one sentence in the brief, in the language of the post, and say where it sits so a viewer cannot miss it. Collegiate Agency checks the live post against that sentence. Your counsel owns the wording. If the proposal folds the posts into the event fee, split them back out before you sign.

## What should you refuse before you sign?

Refuse one national price. Refuse a reach number with no campus on it. Refuse a view total, a coupon rate, or a redemption forecast copied from another company's campus work. Refuse a format the sheet does not name. Refuse a student club billed at $1,500 per sorority (chapter) when it is not a sorority (chapter). Refuse a paid post with no disclosure sentence. Refuse a campus outside the published network of 21 unless that school has its own yes.
