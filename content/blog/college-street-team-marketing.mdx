The team staffs a table, walks a route, or hands out a unit, and the brief names the count you will accept. ## What does a college street team do on one shift?

The hero photograph shows a NUTRL bar setup Collegiate Agency photographed: a banner, stocked cans, and a bartender holding a can. The bartender works the venue. She is not the street team.

On a table shift, students stay with the table and record the action you named. On a route, they move only through places you listed. On a handout, the job is the unit leaving their hands. A talk without the unit is not the count. Tabling, walking, and posting in one hour are three jobs. Split them before you sign. A larger crew at an event you do not control belongs in the [campus event staffing agency guide](/insights/campus-event-staffing-agency). That guide turns an event flow into positions. This page is the shift.

Table: What a brand names on a college street team brief

| Piece | What you name | Who owns the yes | Send it back if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Team size | Students on that shift | You set the job | No number |
| Shift | Campus, date, and hours | You, on that calendar | A week with no school |
| Job | Table, route, or handout | You | No action named |
| Script | Sentences they may say | You write, they train | A mood, no sentences |
| Permission | The office, and what the yes covers | Campus or event owner | We know students there |
| Count | The one number in the recap | You | A photo called attendance |

Do not sign a price that skips a row. A second campus needs its own row. A yes at one school does not travel.

## How many students should the shift include?

Name the number. Two students can cover a short table if one can step away to restock. More students on a route can cover more doors, and they can drift or block a walkway if nobody owns the map. The right number matches the job, the space, and the count. Collegiate Agency does not print that number as a package.

Here is an example, not a client result. A beverage brand wants cans in hands outside one student union for three hours, with four students, one lead on the count sheet, and a unit count at the end. Posts the next day stay on the ambassador line at $150 per ambassador per month. One price that hides both is the proposal to send back.

Hours sit with the headcount. Ask who arrives, who covers a gap, and who can stop the shift. A name on last night's roster is not a student on the ground at the start. Before work begins, the lead reads the sentences, shows the unit, and shows the count sheet. A student who has not heard the script does not start. Weather, a closed door, a missing shipment, or an officer who moves the table are reasons to pause. The lead calls the person you named. The team does not pick a new spot.

## What permission and what script come before the team starts?

Permission sits with the campus or the event owner, not with a photograph and not with a student who says they know someone. Ask which office can approve a table, a route, or a handout, what the yes covers, and how far ahead the request has to be in. A yes for one quad is not a yes for the student union. A yes for a table is not a yes to walk the sidewalk. A yes at one school is not a yes at the next.

American University, through its Division of Student Affairs, issued a [University Policy: Tabling Policy](https://www.american.edu/policies/students/upload/tabling-policy-final-2025.pdf) effective October 15, 2025. It covers recognized student organizations, university departments and programs, and outside groups an American University host sponsors. A host reserves the table at least 72 hours ahead. An outside group gets a table only through that host, who handles the schedule, the contract, the permission, and the payment, and who is responsible for the content and for guests who are not part of the university. Displays stay within 2 to 3 feet of the table. The group stays the full reservation, keeps the assigned spot, and does not block walkways. Amplified sound is not allowed. This is one school's rule, read on November 24, 2026. It is not a Collegiate Agency rule. Do not copy those 72 hours onto another school. A student ID is not a permit, and that page is about a reserved table, not a walking route.

Write the script before the shift. Include the product name, the one action, and the words the team may not say. A claim about health, price, or a result has to be a sentence you already approved. Age rules and storage rules go in before the first hour.

The Federal Trade Commission adopted revised [Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm) on July 26, 2023, effective that day. A material connection needs to be disclosed when a significant minority of the audience does not expect it. Clear and conspicuous means difficult to miss and easily understood. On social media the disclosure should be unavoidable. A disclosure only on a profile page is easy to miss, and a disclosure behind a "more" control is not unavoidable when the post itself is visible. In the examples, a college student earns points for posts and can exchange them for prizes such as concert tickets or electronics. Those incentives have to be disclosed.

Pay, free product, or a prize is a material connection when listeners would not expect it. Put that disclosure in the words the student will say. A post needs its own disclosure. A sentence at the table does not cover a post a viewer can see without it. Collegiate Agency checks the live post against the brief. Your counsel owns the wording.

## What should the recap be allowed to count?

Count only the action named for that campus and that shift. That can be units handed out, students present at the start, the permission sent before the hour, and the script they were trained on. Collegiate Agency will not add a number you did not ask for, and it will not publish another company's totals as its own result.

## What should you ask before you hire the team?

Ask these questions before you compare price. Each one maps to a row in the table.

- How many students are on this shift, and who is the lead.
- What time does it start and end, and what happens if someone does not arrive.
- Is the job a table, a route, or a handout, and what number will the recap show.
- Which office already said yes, and what did that yes cover.
- Which sentences may they say, and where does the disclosure sit.
- Is this the events package, the ambassador month, or both, named separately.
